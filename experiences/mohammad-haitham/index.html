    A STUDENT’S STEP 1 JOURNEY

    A timeline with room for deeper learning

    MH
    Mohammad HaithamEnglish · 7 min read · Community archive

    A historical account of a 250 score, structured preparation, and learning through question explanations.

    PlanningQuestion practice
    Author-reported outcome: 250 (historical scored exam). Not independently verified. Original language and wording are preserved; the title and introduction are editorial. Advice and exam policies may be outdated.

    Step 1 Score: 250

    Target: 250+

    Exam date: January 19, 2022. (During my sixth year)

    My name is Mohammad Haitham, ومن أحياها batch. I thought of sharing my Step 1 experience. I hope it will be as useful as reading other’s experience was to me!

    Before explaining my journey, I would like to note a couple of points:

    -I took the exam before the Pass and Fail thing, so you will notice that I used many resources during step 1 preparation process, many of them might be not that important for Step 1 exam (don’t be overwhelmed by them) but keep in mind that the more effort you put on step 1 the easier step 2 preparation would be and the more general knowledge you will end up having.

    - Don’t ever study without a TIMELINE, random studying can never end, try to stick to a timeline as much as possible.

    -Everyone has his own experience, just do whatever fits you.

    General: Ever since I started my second basic year, my aim was to go through USMLE steps pathway, so during my second and third basic year I used to study the university system syllabus from textbooks such as BRS physiology, Pathoma, High yield anatomy, and Kaplan anatomy, microbiology and biochemistry (In addition to doctors’ slides). After each system, I used to revise the material from FA and Pathoma, so I ended up getting high scores during med school and a pretty good basic knowledge.

    Side note: Don’t ever finish your fourth medical year without memorizing Pathoma by heart. It will help you in USMLE in addition to your clinical rotations.

    Actual preparation (10-11 months with many ons and offs, 6-7 studying hours):

    First day of my 5th year was day 1 of this journey when I started FA in addition to Boards and beyond simultaneously. BnB is a very High yield source for understanding every word in FA and adding up more knowledge and small details that are high yield yet not mentioned in FA (you probably should take notes!!). BnB also highlights some keys for each concept.

    This took me around 5-6 months. During the same period, I used pathoma after each subject I was done with and used the above mentioned resources for the concepts I didn’t fully get.

    After that, I started UWorld question bank. Each time I start a new system block, I would revise the general concepts from my annotated FA. During UWorld sessions, READ every word in the explanation, add notes to your book and be generous while using the mark tool so you can go over these directly and re-do the marked and wrong ones, so you will not need to do UWorld twice.

    During the same period, I used Amboss library and question bank (I loved it) after each chapter, I ended up finishing all Amboss question bank. There are many questions in-common between UWorld and Amboss, but you can find new ideas and remember, the more questions, the more test taking skills you gain.

    -Note: UWorld is enough as a question bank, Amboss is not a must (except for Ethics), you can only use it if you need more questions or to strengthen your weaknesses.

    **You can take one self-assessment at the end of this period, so you know what areas you need to work on.

    Dedicated period: (2 months);

    I studied 10-11 hours daily (it was COVID-19) in the beginning and 14 hours/day towards the end. Things you must do during this time: FA final reads, random marked and timed UW blocks in the morning (Mark again questions that you find tricky and focus on time management skills), NBME’s and UWorld assessments, videos of the 1st 3 chapters of Pathoma (extremely high yield).

    **I also did Ethics and Biostatics during this period for the first time ever (which I think is a mistake).

    Resources:

    First aid is the absolute bible for this test!! It covers almost 80-85% of the exam content! So make sure to memorize every single word of it. I did almost 4 full revisions.

    UWorld is VERY VERY important and if I had to choose one resource, I would choose UWorld because UWorld will teach you medical knowledge AND test taking skills.

    ***Remember that UWorld is an educational tool, don’t get pissed off if you had low scores in the beginning.

    As I mentioned above, I also used Boards and beyond, Pathoma and Amboss for nearly everything.

    For biochemistry and genetics, I also used Kaplan. It’s the best review book to study biochemistry.

    For neurology BnB and UWorld is enough for the exam, but I also watched Kaplan videos (Dr. James white) HE IS A GENIUS!

    For Biostat: Randy Neil (Youtube) +Amboss question bank. (+Uworld and FA for sure)

    Anatomy is not that High yield, just stick with FA and UWorld pictures.

    Ethics: The biggest problem for IMGs, and it was the biggest problem for me. Ethics can be very tricky, 100 cases by Conrad Fischer was useless for me, so I had to do all Amboss ethics question banks and “Dirty medicine” which helped me a lot. (In addition to UWorld)

    *Used anki from time to time, didn’t like it that much, Microbiology anki was good though.

    Remember: A huge part of my preparation was to gain knowledge, that’s why I studied many unnecessary resources for step 1. Don’t be overwhelmed by them. You only need FA and UWorld to pass the exam easily. (but study them well)

    Self-assessments:

    **Only Use them to track your progress in your weak areas only! I read many experiences before, many of them had scores that are different from most of their nmbes.

    UWorld first pass: 83%

    NBME 25-30: NBME 25 was my first one (1.5 month before the exam) and scored 243 . The rest of the NBMEs were between 253-258.

    UWorld assessment 1: 265

    UWorld assessment 2: 269

    *UWorld assessments were over predictive for me.

    And since It is a pass/fail exam, I think +210 is good enough to pass the exam!

    **Important points:

    1-Purchase UWorld as soon as possible. (After your first FA read)

    2-Better have a study partner (Shout out to my study buddy Hussam Barham).

    3-Since it is pass/fail, Take the exam during your clinical years and as soon as possible (try not to take it after graduation).

    4-The exam is similar to the UWorld style (not NBME’s).

    5-During any self-assessment, prepare yourself just like it is the real exam, get up in the morning, have a healthy breakfast, and set a timer, since they are offline and don’t take long breaks.

    6-Step 1 is highly condensed material, and needs a lot of revisions, if you had final exams or any other circumstances, try not to leave FA for a long time, you will forget everything.

    7-During med school try to implement the USMLE materials in your study schedule as much as you can.

    8-Exam Day performance matters a lot, so follow “dirty medicine” recommendations on his Youtube channel.

    Feel free to ask any question, Good luck everyone!
